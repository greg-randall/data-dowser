How is this ranked?

This list ranks every public water system in Texas from worst to best based on the severity of drinking-water violations.

Severity Score

For every violation, we take how much contaminant showed up and divide it by the legal limit, then add them together.

In the case of Silver Creek in 2014 they had 345 units of Radium where the limit is 5, so that one test alone gives them a severity of 69x (345/5=69). That then gets added to a bunch of other violations to get them to 360.21.

Since some systems have been around for 15 years and others only 2, we also show Annual Score. This is the total severity divided by the number of years the system has been active, giving you a better sense of their yearly track record.

Impact

Impact is the score that asks how bad is this at a population level. It multiplies Severity by the population the system serves.

A tiny town with god-awful violations can actually score lower on Impact than a bigger place with milder problems—because fewer people are exposed. It’s what keeps the small-town nightmares from getting lost in the shuffle.

The Composite Score

The Composite Score blends Severity and Impact into one number.

To keep extreme outliers from dominating the rankings, both values are scaled before being combined. The final score is weighted 70% toward Severity and 30% toward Impact. It’s a single metric designed to highlight which systems have the most serious, real-world water problems right now.

* Systems with an Unknown population are ranked based on their Severity Score only.

A Note on the Data

We only fixed one obvious typo from 2016 in Fayette County that was 4,500× higher than everything else that year. Everything else is straight from the state reports.

Note: some top entries are driven by a single extreme reading likely caused by a data-entry error in the upstream TCEQ document. The "worst violation" column shows each system's peak so outliers are easy to spot.
